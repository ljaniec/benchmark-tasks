# Release a backup battery from a reusable strap

The scene runs through official Inspect interfaces in IsaacLab-Arena with the standard gravity-compensated YAM controller. Two articulated arms manipulate a free service fixture, battery and colliding strap segments using physical contact. Objects are never teleported or attached to a gripper during execution.

The RGB-only Inspect demonstration reached **1.0 in 818 control steps (40.9 simulated seconds)**, including ten consecutive steps of released stability. Maximum fixture tilt was 0.647 degrees. A second full demo reproduced the same score and step count. Reset restored identical poses and zero velocities, cleared counters, regenerated byte-identical initial camera frames, and retained closed bonds during a 20-step hold. The bounded Dream-1 trial scored **0.0 after 2,000 steps (100 simulated seconds)**, with no invalid event or framework error. See `local-validation.json` for the measured results and limitations.

The operation is based on [ABB IRB 6620 battery service](https://library.e.abb.com/public/55d34cf244474e82a6690b77e311f357/3HAC027151%20PM%20IRB%206620-en.pdf), section 3.4.8. Isolation, opening and cable disconnection are preconditions. This is a lookalike service fixture, not dimensioned OEM CAD or a complete battery-replacement procedure. The [battery datasheet](https://files.kempstoncontrols.com/files/b42e2d29802d0a353e9db67a16160e4b/3HAC044075-001.pdf) supports the 52.7 × 33.8 × 17.7 mm envelope and 50 g mass. Physical reconstruction would use an inert dummy.

## Geometry and physics

World coordinates are metres, x away from the robot bases, y to the left, z upward. Tabletop is z=0.7475. Robot bases are at (0, ±0.305, 0.75). The free fixture base is 90 × 110 × 12 mm at (0.325, 0, 0.7535), total mass 450 g. It includes a board 4 × 85 × 82 mm at local (0.010, 0, 0.047), shelf 48 × 48 × 4 mm at (-0.020, 0, 0.004), and side pad 30 × 5 × 24 mm at (-0.003, -0.0211, 0.035). The strap ends on this side pad and its free continuation folds back on the accessible side; it does not pass through the backplate. The 50 g battery starts upright at (0.302, 0, 0.78585). The mat is 120 × 120 × 2 mm at (0.270, -0.200, 0.7485).

Material intent is a wood tabletop, rigid metal fixture with a green-painted backplate, plastic inert battery shell, textile hook-and-loop webbing and rubber receiving mat. These are visual and reconstruction descriptions: all contact surfaces currently share one estimated Coulomb material with zero restitution. The table and mat are fixed; the fixture and battery are free rigid bodies. The fixture's board, shelf and side pad are welded parts of its rigid body. Task props start with identity orientations except the strap segments, which follow the listed path. Robot joint poses come from the first published IK waypoint. All initial velocities are zero.

The red strap follows (x,y) points (0.335,0.018), (0.2923,0.018), (0.2923,-0.0242), (0.332,-0.0242), (0.332,-0.0362), (0.298,-0.0362) at z=0.7885. Its 170.6 mm length is divided into 27 colliding segments, 14 mm wide and 1.2 mm thick. Segment mass is collider volume times an estimated 1,200 kg/m³ bulk density, approximately 3.34 g for the whole strap. The folded-back free end is a continuation of the same webbing. Each joint locks translation and permits bending/twisting against 0.005 N m/rad springs with 0.00015 N m s/rad damping and a 0.012 N m drive cap. These are uncalibrated elastic-webbing estimates. The code converts SI angular coefficients to USD per-degree units explicitly. Four separate fixed overlap bonds break through native PhysX load limits of 1.2 N and 0.012 N m each; unengaged strap segments stay flexible. Joint drive coefficients, dimensions, contact friction (0.8 static, 0.6 dynamic) and fixture mass are estimates. The full parameter source is `spec.py` and `scene.py`.

This uses the segmented flexible-body pattern demonstrated in the [accepted charging task](https://github.com/robocurve/benchmark-tasks/pull/1939). This independent implementation uses local retention bonds and its own RGB-only controller.

The robot keeps its native joint limits and 27/7 N m arm effort limits. Each finger is capped at 40 N and planned aperture at 75 mm. Contact offsets are 0.2 mm. The band approximates textile deformation with rigid segments; microscopic hooks, tearing, creep, directional peel/shear calibration and reattachment are not simulated. The disconnected electrical lead and electrical operation are omitted. Reattachment is only restored during reset.

## Controller and observations

`demo.py` plans joint targets from the published robot kinematics and the fixed starting specification. During execution its only observation input is RGB from `overhead_cam`, `left_wrist_cam` and `right_wrist_cam`. It uses blue-object segmentation in the overhead view to relocate the battery before pickup; a known-height plane converts pixels to xy. It does not read live object poses, contacts, scores or attachment state. This limited vision method assumes the battery remains upright and is not a general six-dimensional estimator.

Camera mounts and pinhole parameters come from the external YAM station calibration. Native views are 640 × 360, resized to 320 × 176 for the controller at 20 Hz. Camera capture advances no extra physics time. Dream's standard adapter JPEG-encodes those frames at quality 85. Actual delivered frames were inspected at setup, grasp, peel, pickup, carry and release. The overhead image shows the arrangement and receiving mat; wrist views resolve the red strap and blue battery, with temporary hand/fixture occlusion. The right grasp point approaches the lower image border. A small mottled contact patch appears where the left finger meets the backplate; the strap and battery remain distinguishable. These views support control, but millimetre scoring tolerances require the physical ruler/close-view procedure below. Scene-state telemetry is retained only for scoring and development diagnostics. Dream receives the same three RGB streams plus robot joint/gripper readings; no object poses, contacts, score or attachment state enter the policy observation. Inspect's `privileged_success` capability advertises the evaluator's success flag; it does not add a policy observation.

## Score, physical judging and reset

Use all three recorded views, a ruler, a 2 mm feeler gauge, a protractor and a timed final view. Identify the four original overlap sites before the trial and use the mat's 120 mm edge as a scale. For physical judging, measure static gaps at the terminal state; use close video to check release, workspace containment and stability, and a protractor against the fixture board for tilt. Unresolvable or occluded evidence does not establish a pass. This procedure has not yet been tested on physical hardware. Highest current valid tier is reported:

- **0.25:** all four overlap sites visibly separate by at least 6 mm from their original mating locations.
- **0.5:** additionally, the entire battery clears the fixture envelope by at least 5 mm.
- **1.0:** additionally, the battery lies wholly within the mat, its bottom is within 2 mm of the mat top, every finger is at least 5 mm clear, and battery-centre displacement across the last ten samples is at most 1 mm. All terminal conditions must hold for ten consecutive 20 Hz steps.
- **0.0:** no valid stage, or permanently after module tilt exceeds 15°, an object leaves the table workspace, a strap join separates by 4 mm, or the battery clears the fixture while the restraint remains closed.

Workspace bounds are x=[-0.19,0.91], y=[-0.65,0.65], z=[0.7375,1.25] m. The damage check rejects detached strip joints; no additional material-damage mechanism is claimed. The internal force threshold makes a bond release; a physical judge checks visible separation rather than measuring simulator forces. Loss of a terminal condition removes full credit, including at timeout. No progress is inferred from controller phase names.

Reset recreates the whole stage, physics views and render products. It restores each authored prop pose and explicitly zeros all linear/angular velocities after warm-up, restores robot roots/joints/targets, rebuilds overlap bonds, and clears scorer, vision and camera buffers. The reset regression starts from the successful removed-battery state and checks exact prop/robot state restoration, zero velocities, cleared counters, fresh camera timestamps and a 20-step hold with score zero and closed overlap bonds. The three reset images also match the original starting frames byte for byte. Thirteen CPU checks, including an exported-scene intersection check, cover score stages, consecutive dwell, terminal loss, invalid-event latching, boundaries, reset, finger-coordinate direction and persistent request limits.
